# AI Champion

A good AI Champion is someone capable of combining two complementary roles:
- Drives adoption of AI tools and practices that improve the project team's overall productivity and efficiency.
- Tracks emerging AI-SDLC trends, evaluates their effectiveness, recognizes those with potential value, and experiments with the most promising approaches within their project.

## Delivery Manager vs. AI Champion Focus and Accountability

**AI Champion**
- Owns the technical aspect of the AI maturity journey.
- Selects and configures the right AI tools for the project.
- Defines efficient AI-driven ways of working and ensures they are practical for all roles.
- Creates and maintains clear guidelines for effective tool usage.

**Delivery Manager / Project Manager / Scrum Master**
- Responsible for team enablement and delivery alignment with AI adoption goals.
- Encourages the team to follow the AI Champion's recommendations.
- Facilitates client approvals for required tools and AI-related initiatives.
- Resolves blockers that hinder AI maturity progress.

## How Many AI Champions a Project Needs

### For Level 2
At least 1 champion that represents some roles on the project.

### For Level 3
It is expected that AI Champion(s) collectively represent the core roles. In some cases a project may require more than 1 Champion. Champions must collectively cover all relevant domains and collaborate to uplift the entire project to the required maturity level. The DM/PM/SM is responsible for defining each Champion's area of responsibility and ensuring productive coordination between them.

## Skills Required for an AI Champion

### AI Foundations & Responsible Use
1. Understanding the nature, scope of application, and key constraints of GenAI.
2. Awareness of the distinct use cases of major LLMs and their comparative strengths.
3. Awareness of security, compliance, and ethical issues associated with GenAI usage in SDLC.
4. Understanding of cost optimization techniques, incl. prompt efficiency, caching, model selection, and workload balancing.

### Prompt & Context Engineering
1. Understanding context-engineering techniques, prompt structures, and reusable prompt patterns.
2. Creating and maintaining shared rules/instructions, and shared prompt libraries for consistent use across teams.
3. Promoting team-wide consistency and reusability in AI-driven workflows.

### Tool & Agent Integration
1. Configuring and using AI coding tools for code generation, refactoring, and review.
2. Configuring AI assistants for other use cases, incl. requirements gathering, user story mapping, technical documentation creation, and test cases generation and documentation.
3. Integrating AI assistants with external platforms, such as Jira, Confluence, CI/CD, and diagramming tools.
4. Understanding the basic capabilities of AI agent platforms and integrating them into SDLC.
5. Ability to identify and manage the limitations of AI tools to ensure high-quality and reliable outputs.
6. Knowing and applying the hottest features, such as workflows, CLI agents and background agents, agent integration into CI/CD pipelines, and emerging frameworks such as Spec-Kit or BMAD.

### Advanced AI Systems & Optimization
1. Deep understanding of the SDLC and ways to optimize its processes using the latest AI tools.
2. Knowledge of multi-agent communication patterns and the ability to design coordinated multi-agent systems.
3. Understanding fine-tuning and specialization of LLMs or AI assistants for team- or domain-specific contexts.
4. Expertise in creating custom AI toolkits tailored to specific project needs.
5. Ability to monitor AI infrastructure performance and maintain systems for long-term stability and scalability.

## AI Champion Activities

- **Identify Opportunities** — Partner with DM/PM/SM to map value streams and pinpoint use cases where AI can deliver measurable impact.
- **Select and Plan Tools** — Evaluate suitable AI tools for each use case, plan their implementation, and actively request input from all key roles (including BAs, QAs, and Devs).
- **Develop Artefacts** — Take ownership of creating the first version of shareable artefacts — such as agents, prompt libraries, rules/instruction files, and skill files — and ensure their integration into team workflows.
- **Collect and Refine** — Support DM/PM/SM in gathering feedback and challenges, and in documenting success stories and proven practices for sharing across the team.
- **Promote Knowledge Sharing** — Spread best practices, prompt/context engineering tips, and AI usage optimization techniques across the team.
- **Foster Innovation** — Encourage experimentation with advanced AI features and help define strategies for deeper AI integration and workflow optimization.
