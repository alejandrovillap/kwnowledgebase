# SDLC Discovery

Imagine building a house without a blueprint — that's essentially what software development would be like without the discovery phase. This is the initial and critical stage of the software development life cycle (SDLC), where the groundwork for the project is laid out. It aims to clarify project objectives, understand stakeholder needs, outline the necessary requirements, and establish a clear scope. This phase sets the stage for successful project planning and execution by defining user expectations, identifying potential risks, and aligning the project with business goals.

## Stakeholder Identification

Who has a vested interest in the project? Understanding their needs and expectations is crucial.

Stakeholder identification involves finding everyone impacted by or interested in a project's success. This includes internal teams (developers, project managers) and external parties (end users, customers, investors, regulators, and potential partners). Early stakeholder understanding is crucial for clear communication, well-informed decisions, and a higher chance of project success.

**Key roles**: Business analysts, product managers, and project manager.

### Challenges in the Traditional Approach

**Identifying All Key Players**: In large projects with limited information, identifying all key players can be challenging, leading to missed voices and incomplete stakeholder landscapes.

**Keeping Up with Evolving Projects**: Traditional methods might struggle to keep pace with evolving projects (changing requirements, new stakeholders), leading to outdated stakeholder lists and missed collaboration opportunities.

## Discovery Activities

- **Requirements Elicitation**: What functionalities should the software have? Gathering requirements from stakeholders.
- **Market and Competitive Analysis**: What similar products exist? Understanding the market landscape helps position the project.
- **Technical Feasibility Assessment**: Can this project be built with current technology and resources?
- **Risk Identification and Analysis**: What are potential roadblocks, and how can we mitigate them?
- **Project Scope Definition**: What features will be included, and what will be left out?
- **Business Case Development**: Justify the project's value by outlining its benefits and return on investment (ROI).
- **Definition of Personas and User Journeys**: Who are the target users, and what are their needs and experiences?
