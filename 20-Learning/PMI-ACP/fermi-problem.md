# Fermi problem

Fermi problems are estimation challenges that require making reasonable assumptions to arrive at approximate answers, often used in physics and engineering education.

## Definition and Purpose

A **Fermi problem** (or Fermi question) is an estimation problem named after physicist **Enrico Fermi**, known for his ability to make good approximate calculations with little or no actual data. These problems typically involve making justified guesses about quantities and their variances, often leading to "back-of-the-envelope" calculations. The goal is not to find an exact answer but to estimate an order of magnitude that is "close enough to be useful".

## Educational Use

Fermi problems are widely used in educational settings to develop students' problem-solving skills and mathematical reasoning. They encourage students to break down complex questions into manageable parts, make reasonable assumptions, and justify their estimates. For example, a common Fermi problem might ask, "How many leaves are on all the trees in Central Park?" Students would estimate the number of trees, the average number of leaves per tree, and then calculate the total.

## Examples of Fermi Problems

- How many piano tuners are there in Chicago?
- How many tennis balls would it take to fill a classroom?
- How many liters of water does a school use in a year?
- How many times do you blink in a day?

## Approach to Solving Fermi Problems

1. **Define the Question**: Clearly state what you are trying to estimate.
2. **Break It Down**: Decompose the problem into smaller, more manageable parts.
3. **Make Assumptions**: Use reasonable assumptions based on available knowledge or common sense.
4. **Calculate**: Perform the calculations based on your estimates and assumptions.
5. **Refine**: Review your estimates and calculations to see if they make sense and adjust if necessary.

Fermi problems are valuable for developing critical thinking and estimation skills. They help individuals learn to navigate uncertainty and make informed decisions based on limited information.
