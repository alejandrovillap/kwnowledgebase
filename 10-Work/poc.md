# PoC — Proof of Concept

A **Proof of Concept (POC)** is the process of gathering evidence to support the feasibility of a project. Project managers perform a POC in the early stages of development before committing too much time and resources.

The purpose is to demonstrate project viability to product teams, clients, and other stakeholders. A POC may uncover flaws — leading the company to revise or abandon a project — or confirm the likelihood of success.

## Why PoC Is Important

- **Finite resources**: Tests feasibility, ensuring companies pursue only viable projects.
- **Enhanced trust**: Provides evidence of viability, earning trust among stakeholders and investors.
- **Better planning**: Identifies roadblocks and informs project direction before they arise.

## Key Components of a PoC

- **Problem statement**: Describe what challenges the project will solve.
- **Project definition**: Define what the project intends to do.
- **Project goals**: Outline intended outcomes and how you will measure them.
- **Required resources**: List tools and resources needed to produce results.

## How to Create a PoC in 7 Steps

**Step 1: Define your project idea** — Identify the project to test. What problems will it solve, and for whom?

**Step 2: Set your success criteria** — Decide on benchmarks to measure success or failure. If for a client, consult them about how they define success.

**Step 3: List the resources you will need** — Build an exhaustive list of tangible and intangible resources (material goods, technology, tools, human capital).

**Step 4: Determine your timeline** — Create a product roadmap from ideation to development. Will there be a full launch from the beginning or staged scaling?

**Step 5: Develop and test your prototype** — Once project scope is decided, develop and test with the target audience. Gather all feedback, both positive and negative.

**Step 6: Review and refine** — Evaluate how the prototype performed against success criteria. Conduct a competitive analysis. Use findings to improve areas that fall short.

**Step 7: Present your PoC** — Present to stakeholders for development approval. Provide a clear model of how the idea works. Emphasize how it addresses pain points and meets audience needs.

## PoC vs Prototype vs MVP

- **PoC**: Determines project *feasibility* — can this be built at all?
- **Prototype**: A draft version of the final product to test design, usability, and functionality.
- **MVP (Minimum Viable Product)**: A market-ready solution for users; an improved version of the prototype tested further to create the final product.

## PoC in Different Industries

**Software development**: Identifies which technology or framework best supports the project in meeting its success criteria.

**Pharmaceuticals**: Assesses efficacy requirements and includes a cost-benefit analysis to ensure profit.
